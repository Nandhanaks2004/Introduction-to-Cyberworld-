Security Model
Never Trust.
Always Verify.

Zero Trust is a security philosophy built on one premise: assume breach. No user, device, or network is trusted by default — even inside the perimeter.

Think of it like airport security — valid passengers are verified every single time. No exceptions. No assumptions.

Incident Reports

Real attacks.
Real victims.

These are not hypothetical scenarios. They happen every minute, to real people, in the real world.

⚠ INCOMING THREAT
INCIDENT 001
Phishing Attack
The Urgent Email
Employee receives "Urgent: Password Reset Required" — from what appears to be IT support
Clicks the link without thinking. Urgency bypasses critical thinking.
Lands on a pixel-perfect replica of the company login page
Types credentials — stolen in milliseconds. Attacker now has access.
Entire company network compromised. No malware. No exploit. Just human error.
"Hackers don't always hack systems. Sometimes they hack humans."
INCIDENT 002
Social Engineering
The Helpful Stranger
Fake "IT support" calls the employee during a busy afternoon
Creates panic: "Your account is being accessed right now." Fear activates.
Builds credibility using researched company details and insider vocabulary
Victim shares OTP to "verify identity." The attacker had already triggered a login.
Bank account drained within minutes. No malware involved at any point.
"Humans are the weakest link in any security system…"
"…but educated humans become the strongest defense."
Attacker Perspective

How attackers think.

To defend against attacks, you must understand how they're structured. Every breach follows a recognizable pattern.

PHASE 01
Reconnaissance
Mapping the target. Gathering intelligence from public sources.
PHASE 02
Weaponize
Crafting the exploit. Phishing emails, fake pages, malicious payloads.
PHASE 03
Access
Gaining entry through the identified vulnerability — human or technical.
PHASE 04
Persist
Establishing presence. Creating backdoors. Avoiding detection.
PHASE 05
Impact
Data exfiltration, encryption, destruction, or silent long-term surveillance.

Field Guide

Your digital
survival rules.

Simple habits that separate secure people from vulnerable ones.

Practice these
01Enable Multi-Factor Authentication on every account — especially email and banking
02Hover over links to preview the destination URL before clicking anything
03Keep all systems and applications updated — patches close known attack paths
04Use a password manager to generate and store unique credentials per site
05Pause before acting on urgent requests — verify through official channels first
06Verify callers claiming to be IT or support by calling back on official numbers
Avoid these
01Reuse the same password across multiple platforms — one breach exposes everything
02Share OTPs, passwords, or recovery codes with anyone — including "IT support"
03Download files or software from unknown, unverified, or untrusted sources
04Dismiss or delay security update notifications — they exist for critical reasons
05Overshare job details, tools, or routines on social media — attackers profile you
06Act impulsively when someone creates artificial urgency or panic — that's the trap
